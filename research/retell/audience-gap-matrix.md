# Retell AI — Audience Evidence & Gap Matrix

## Legend

- **A1:** independent source with a named customer
- **P1:** Retell product, legal, pricing or job-process evidence
- **C1:** attributable named customer in a Retell-hosted case
- **R1:** review evidence
- **M1:** community evidence
- **I1:** researcher inference grounded in listed evidence

Review and community evidence are never treated as prevalence. Retell-hosted customer outcomes are attributed, not audited.

## ICP and role claims

| Claim | Status | Evidence | Confidence | Caveat / missing evidence | Safe writing use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Best fit is defined more by call-workflow economics than employee count. | Supported synthesis | A1/C1 | High | No customer distribution by size or volume. | Say observed customers span SMB to enterprise, united by material phone-workflow pain. |
| Direct operators are one core customer motion. | Supported | A1/C1 | High | Named cases are selected successes. | Describe support, scheduling, collections, admissions, lead and help-desk examples. |
| Vertical SaaS / embedded operators are a distinct motion. | Supported | C1/B1 | High | Revenue share and customer count unavailable. | Use Cents and Spare as concrete examples. |
| Agencies, BPOs and certified partners are a distinct motion. | Supported | C1/P1/R1 | High | Public review population may overrepresent agencies. | Describe multi-client implementation and operation, not market share. |
| High or volatile call volume is a strong qualifier. | Supported | A1/C1 | High | No universal minimum volume. | Tie need to a named baseline, not an invented threshold. |
| A bounded, recoverable first workflow improves fit. | Supported | A1/C1/P1 | High | “Bounded” remains workload-specific. | Recommend narrow scope plus human escalation. |
| A system-of-record integration is usually necessary for task completion. | Supported | A1/C1/P1 | High | Simple FAQ/reception agents may need less integration. | Say value increases when the agent can read/write operational systems. |
| A human fallback is structurally important. | Supported | A1/C1/P1 | High | Exact escalation model varies. | Treat handoff as a production requirement for exceptions. |
| Ongoing testing and QA ownership is necessary. | Supported | A1/C1/P1/M1 | High | No benchmark for staffing or test coverage. | Cite Pine Park, Matic and Spare behavior. |
| Global eligibility depends on telephony, data region and law. | Supported | P1/M1 | High | Exact provider routing and negotiated terms vary. | Ask buyers to verify destination, number, processing and contract before build. |
| “Any business that answers a phone” is the ICP. | Rejected | I1 | High | It is a marketing universe, not observed qualification. | Do not use. |
| Enterprise-only ICP. | Rejected | C1/R1 | High | Enterprise named cases are prominent. | Include SMB/agency and embedded motions. |
| SMB-only/self-serve ICP. | Rejected | A1/C1/P1 | High | Review corpus skews small/agency. | Include governed enterprise motion. |

| Technical Owner is standalone. | Supported | C1/P1 | High | Can combine with builder in a small team. | Separate architecture/production authority from hands-on configuration. |
| Agent Builder is standalone. | Supported | C1/P1/R1 | High | Review sample is agency-heavy. | Describe direct prompt/flow/tool/test work. |

| Governance Approver is standalone. | Supported mainly as a process/gate | P1/M1 | Medium-high | No named customer security/legal/procurement speaker. | Present as a required function in applicable deals, not a guaranteed seat. |
| Solution Partner & Platform Operator is standalone. | Supported | C1/P1/R1 | High | Partners and embedded SaaS have different contracts. | Group by multi-client repeatability/economics, then show variants. |
| Separate procurement persona. | Not sufficiently supported | P1 | Medium | No named Retell-specific procurement workflow. | Merge into Governance Approver; note sponsor owns value. |

## Journey and proof claims

| Claim | Status | Evidence | Confidence | Caveat / missing evidence | Safe writing use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem → discovery/business case → technical validation → security/procurement → commercial/executive alignment is Retell's intended enterprise sequence. | Supported seller process | P1 | High for intended motion | No observed conversion or duration. | Attribute as Retell's enterprise sales process. |
| Pilots should have stakeholders, timeline, success metrics, production requirements and next step. | Supported seller process | P1 | High | No evidence every pilot complies. | Use as a recommended qualification checklist. |
| Land narrowly, manually review, then expand is an observed rollout. | Supported | A1/C1 | High | Selected successes; not universal. | Use Pine Park, TripleTen, Matic and ISpeedToLead examples. |
| Self-serve means implementation is zero-effort. | Rejected | A1/C1/P1/M1 | High | Very simple agents can launch quickly. | Say interface speed and production work are separate. |
| Security, data and communications-law review can stop production. | Supported | P1/M1 | High | Jurisdiction and workload specific. | Give concrete artifacts and unresolved EU-region example. |
| International telephony should be tested before agent work. | Supported | P1/R1/M1 | High | Custom SIP can change feasibility. | Use as early technical qualification. |
| Renewal and expansion owners are known. | Unavailable | — | High | No named renewal, GRR/NRR or contract data. | State the gap; do not infer renewal from roadmap intent. |
| Typical implementation time is one month. | Not representative | A1/C1 | High | Cases range and are selected; scope differs. | Attribute exact times to individual cases only. |

## Customer-language and sentiment claims

| Claim | Status | Evidence | Confidence | Caveat / missing evidence | Safe writing use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ease of setup and natural voice are common review themes. | Supported only as review theme | R1 | Medium | Counts drift; many reviews are incentivized/invited. | Say review summaries/reviewers mention; never claim representative satisfaction. |
| Cost at scale is a recurring review objection. | Supported only as review theme | R1/M1 | Medium | No independent total-cost benchmark. | Explain the component stack and ask for cost per completed outcome. |
| Complex logic/debugging creates friction after easy initial setup. | Supported | R1/M1/P1 | Medium-high | Incident frequency unknown. | Use as evaluation question, not defect-rate claim. |
| Telephony and geography cause production friction. | Supported | R1/M1/P1 | High | Each incident/configuration differs. | Test route, number, SIP and whitelist early. |
| Support is uniformly excellent. | Contradicted | A1/C1/R1 | Medium | Selected named cases praise onboarding; isolated reviews complain. | Say experiences vary and attach the evidence class. |
| Callers universally prefer or cannot detect AI. | Unsupported | C1 | High | Vendor-selected anecdotes only; disclosure may be required. | Do not use. |
| Retell has high customer satisfaction overall. | Unresolved | R1 | Medium | No representative NPS/retention dataset; review acquisition bias. | Attribute platform ratings as snapshots only. |

## Most material missing evidence

1. Win/loss interviews and rejected-opportunity reasons by segment.
2. CRM distribution of champion, buyer, technical evaluator and blocker roles.
3. Sales-cycle and procurement-stage duration.
4. ACV, contract length, budget source and enterprise minimums.
5. Pilot-to-production conversion and failure reasons.
6. Representative implementation time, internal staffing and partner usage.
7. Renewal, churn, GRR, NRR, expansion cohort and named renewal owner.
8. Representative caller and frontline-employee acceptance data.
9. Independent cross-vendor quality, reliability and cost-per-outcome evidence.
10. Exact workload-region/subprocessor routes and an authoritative EU-region roadmap.
