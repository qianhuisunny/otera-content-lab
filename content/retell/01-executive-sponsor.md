# Retell AI Persona 01 — Executive Sponsor

## Context metadata

- **Buying role:** Funds or authorizes a voice-agent program, accepts the strategic exposure, and decides whether evidence justifies production and expansion
- **Representative titles:** CEO, founder, COO, CIO, SVP Digital Transformation, business-unit executive, customer-experience executive
- **Research cutoff:** 2026-09-06
- **Evidence confidence:** High that an executive sponsor recurs in named deployments; medium for exact budget, signature, reporting line, and renewal authority
- **Library context:** [Retell ICP & Buying Context](icp-context.md)

## Evidence convention

- **Independent customer evidence:** A named customer speaking in independent reporting. The reviewed corpus contains one especially strong example: Pine Park Health in Healthcare IT News.
- **Customer statement in a Retell case:** A named customer quote hosted and selected by Retell. It supports what that person said, but not portfolio prevalence or independent validation.
- **Vendor claim:** A result, company attribute, or process stated by Retell without a direct customer quotation or outside corroboration. It is labeled as such.
- **Researcher inference:** A role pattern, motivation, cadence, or organizational dynamic inferred from repeated public evidence. It is a writing hypothesis, not a known private fact.

Do not pool case-study results into a Retell-wide benchmark. Definitions, time periods, denominators, customer populations, and attribution differ.

## Role card

| Dimension | Writing-ready context |
| --- | --- |
| Core mission | Turn a material phone-workflow problem into measurable capacity, service, revenue, or margin improvement without creating uncontrolled customer, legal, or operational exposure. |
| Owns | Strategic priority, investment envelope, acceptable downside, executive alignment, pilot-to-production decision, and expansion or stop decision. |
| Usually influences | First workflow, implementation model, success thresholds, human-service boundary, commercial terms, and executive escalation. |

## Persona in one sentence

The executive sponsor must decide whether Retell can convert a costly or capacity-constrained phone operation into a controlled business capability whose completed outcomes—not merely AI-handled minutes—justify investment and exposure.

### What these cases establish

- The sponsor is defined by authority over the **business exposure and scale decision**, not by one universal title.
- The sponsor may be operational, technical, or founder-led. Public examples include CEO, COO, CIO, and SVP Digital Transformation.
- Named sponsors repeatedly connect the purchase to cost, capacity, service, revenue, or a strategic product opportunity.
- Reviewed named examples involve some combination of builders, workflow experts, quality owners, frontline recipients, or implementation partners. Retell's intended enterprise sales motion also includes technical, security, and procurement review; the public cases do not reveal every full committee.
- Smaller businesses can compress several jobs into one founder or CEO. **Researcher inference:** larger or more regulated deployments are more likely to distribute sponsor, workflow, technical, quality, and approval work, but public evidence does not establish a universal enterprise org pattern.

### What the cases do not establish

- Who controlled the budget or signed each agreement
- A standard reporting line, budget band, ACV, sales cycle, or procurement path
- That a CIO, COO, or CEO always initiates or owns renewal
- A typical Retell ROI, containment rate, time to production, or expansion rate
- That the executive speaker used the Retell workspace personally

## Authority boundaries

### Decisions this role can plausibly own

- Whether the phone problem is important enough to fund now
- Whether the first use case has enough upside and a sufficiently bounded downside
- Which executive metric will govern the program
- How much customer-experience, regulatory, and operational exposure is acceptable
- Whether to staff internally, use a Retell solution partner, or embed Retell in a product—subject to the applicable written partner, platform, reseller, or order-form rights
- Whether pilot evidence clears the threshold for production volume
- Whether the program expands to another workflow, geography, client, or business unit
- Whether the initiative stops when economics or quality fail to hold

### Decisions this role should not make alone

- Which intents the agent may resolve, transfer, reject, or escalate
- Whether a specific prompt, tool call, model, carrier, or integration is production-safe
- What sample and evaluation rubric represent acceptable conversation quality
- Whether data flows, retention, outbound calling, recording, consent, or disclosure satisfy law and internal policy
- Which licensed, clinical, financial, hardship, complaint, or emergency tasks must remain human
- Whether vendor certificates or a successful demo are sufficient evidence for production

### Common role collisions

| Collision | Why it happens | Writing implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sponsor = Workflow Owner | Founder-led or small teams centralize authority and operating knowledge | Speak to one person, but keep the strategic decision and daily operating job logically separate |
| Sponsor = Technical Owner | A CIO or technical founder owns both investment and architecture | Do not substitute technical success for business outcome evidence |
| Sponsor delegates value to IT | Business wants AI but has not assigned a process baseline or outcome owner | Make named workflow ownership a prerequisite, not a post-sale detail |
| Sponsor treats governance as procurement | Legal, privacy, security, and communications obligations surface late | Show the approval path and responsibility map before the pilot becomes politically committed |

## What this person is trying to change

### Jobs to be done

#### Functional jobs

- Convert a rising call-cost or missed-demand curve into scalable completed outcomes
- Add 24/7 or incident-spike capacity without staffing every hour and peak
- Return scarce specialists to work that requires judgment, licensing, empathy, or clinical expertise
- Establish a production AI proof point with an accountable owner and measurable baseline
- Replace an inconsistent BPO, rigid IVR, or brittle internal prototype where the economics justify it
- Create an embedded voice product or client-delivery capability without building the full real-time stack
- Preserve a human path for exceptions while automating repeatable work
- Decide where evidence supports expansion and where autonomy must stop

#### Emotional jobs — researcher inference

- Avoid becoming the executive attached to a visible AI failure or an endless pilot
- Gain confidence that the customer experience will not deteriorate at scale
- Feel that unit economics and downside are understood, not hidden behind a natural-sounding demo
- Reduce anxiety that the program creates a new permanent engineering and support burden

#### Social and organizational jobs — researcher inference

- Be seen as converting AI ambition into a disciplined operating result
- Align business, technology, frontline operations, and risk around one measurable workflow
- Show the board or leadership team progress without overstating autonomy or replacing people indiscriminately
- Give functional teams capacity while preserving clear accountability for callers and outcomes

## Buying triggers

- Support, scheduling, admissions, collections, or service-desk volume grows faster than hiring
- Abandonment, hold time, voicemail, slow lead response, or off-hours leakage becomes economically visible
- A service outage or seasonal peak exposes insufficient burst capacity
- A BPO is expensive, inconsistent, slow to train, or difficult to scale across languages and time zones
- An internal voice build stalls on latency, interruption, telephony, maintenance, or evaluation
- A board, CEO, or transformation mandate asks for one production AI result rather than another demo
- A business unit can finally supply a bounded workflow, baseline, source-system access, and human fallback
- A vertical SaaS company sees a repeatable phone problem across many customers and a new product-revenue opportunity
- A solution partner offers delivery capacity when the customer lacks an internal builder or conversation-operations team

## Current alternatives

These are observed category alternatives or researcher inferences; no public win/loss dataset shows their frequency.

| Alternative | Why the sponsor uses it | Where it can remain better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hire more people or add shifts | Direct control and familiar service model | Low volume, high judgment, sensitive conversations, or workflows that cannot fail safely |
| Outsource to a BPO | Variable capacity, recruiting, training, and geographic coverage | When the buyer needs a managed outcome and lacks technical/operational ownership |
| Improve IVR or callback | Predictable deterministic routing at low complexity | Simple menus, low variability, or strict deterministic requirements |
| Add CCaaS-native automation | Consolidation with an incumbent stack | When suite integration, workforce management, and one-vendor governance matter more than specialist voice tooling |
| Build a voice stack internally | Maximum architectural and product control | Strategic platforms with strong real-time, telephony, ML, reliability, and QA teams |
| Deflect to chat, forms, SMS, or email | Lower synchronous-service cost | Workflows that do not require immediate spoken interaction |
| Accept queues or missed demand | Avoids implementation and governance effort | When the problem is inconvenient but not materially costly |

## Core pains and consequences

### 1. Linear staffing cannot match variable demand

Normal growth, nights and weekends, campaigns, and incidents create different capacity curves. Staffing for the peak is expensive; staffing for the average produces abandonment and missed outcomes.

**Consequence:** cost rises faster than revenue or service capacity, while customers experience delay at the exact moment urgency is highest.

### 2. Human capacity is spent on repeatable calls

Medical assistants, licensed agents, admissions staff, underwriters, and support specialists may spend time on reminders, status, intake, or routing.

**Consequence:** expensive or scarce people have less time for complex work, while leaders may mistakenly frame the project only as headcount reduction.

### 3. A natural voice demo does not prove task completion

The agent must authenticate, retrieve data, take an allowed action, update the source system, transfer with context, and fail safely.

**Consequence:** containment can look strong while downstream completion, customer satisfaction, compliance, or human cleanup remains poor.

### 4. AI economics are easy to understate

The base connected-minute price is not the full cost. Models, voices, telephony, concurrency, QA, optional features, implementation, monitoring, human fallback, and internal support all matter.

**Consequence:** an attractive prototype can become an unplanned variable-cost and operating burden at production volume.

### 5. Brand and regulatory exposure concentrate at the top

Callers experience mistakes directly. Current public [Terms](https://www.retellai.com/legal/terms-of-service) assign material calling, consent, disclosure, recording, content, and legal-compliance duties to the customer; the telecom addendum requires covered consent records to be retained for at least five years. Platform call/chat retention is a different control: Retell's [data-retention documentation](https://docs.retellai.com/accounts/data-retention) says the configurable public default is indefinite. Sensitive-data scope, deletion, and prohibited or unsuitable uses require separate review.

**Consequence:** a poorly bounded deployment can create reputational, contractual, or legal exposure disproportionate to savings.

### 6. Expansion can outrun ownership

A successful first workflow creates demand for more agents, teams, geographies, and downstream customers.

**Consequence:** without technical, quality, workflow, and governance ownership, scale multiplies configuration drift and failure modes rather than value.

## Outcome and KPI scorecard

| KPI family | Sponsor's decision question | Acceptable evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity returned | Are scarce people doing more judgment-intensive work? | Baseline hours/FTE allocation, transferred workload, and post-launch staffing/activity data |
| Service access | Are more callers answered at useful times and acceptable speed? | Answer, wait, abandonment, after-hours completion, and incident-peak performance by cohort |
| Completed outcome | Did the call finish the intended job, not merely stay contained? | Booking, qualified transfer, resolved ticket, collected payment, updated record, or safe escalation |
| Unit economics | Is cost per acceptable completed outcome lower or strategically justified? | Fully configured Retell cost plus implementation, operations, QA, fallback, and avoided cost |
| Customer experience | Did automation preserve or improve trust and service? | CSAT/NPS where method is known, complaint rate, repeat calls, escalation quality, and sampled conversations |
| Risk and control | Are severe failures bounded and obligations owned? | Severe-defect rate, incident record, consent/disclosure evidence, data controls, audit trail, and named approvers |
| Time to value | How quickly did a bounded workflow reach stable production? | Dated baseline, pilot gates, implementation work, production volume, and stabilization period |
| Expansion readiness | Can the pattern repeat without proportional build and support cost? | Second-workflow economics, reusable tests/integrations, operating capacity, and governance pattern |

### Public outcome anchors

- **Independent customer evidence:** Pine Park COO Michael Tadlock reported about 2.1 FTE returned to the Medical Assistant team and a 55.7% booking rate among connected proactive calls. This is the strongest independently reported outcome in the reviewed set.
- **Customer statement in a Retell case:** SWTCH CEO Carter Li attributes more than 50% support-cost reduction to the deployment.
- **Customer statement in a Retell case:** Spare COO Josh Andrews describes roughly 70% average resolution, with simpler customers near 100%.
- **Customer statement in a Retell case:** Everise's Saurabh Sodhani says 65% of voice calls were contained.
- **Customer statement in a Retell case:** Sunshine Loans CEO Dominic Quirk says abandonment moved from 20–30% to about 5–6% and AI handles 75–80% of calls.
- **Vendor claim:** Medical Data Systems handles roughly 30,000 calls and $280,000 in collections monthly with about 70% containment.

These are account-specific anchors. They must not be converted into “Retell customers typically…” copy.

## Workflow and operating-model view

The sponsor does not need to build the agent, but must understand the operating system around it.

| Layer | Executive question | Accountable counterpart |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business workflow | Which intents and outcomes create value, and what remains human? | Workflow Owner |
| Source systems | Can the agent safely retrieve and write CRM, EHR, helpdesk, scheduling, billing, or operations data? | Technical Owner and system owner |
| Conversation implementation | Who turns policy into prompts, flows, tools, and releases? | Agent Builder or solution partner |
| Quality operation | Who reviews calls, maintains evaluations, catches drift, and stops a bad release? | Conversation Quality Owner |
| Human service | Who receives transfers, callbacks, complaints, exceptions, and outages? | Workflow Owner and frontline manager |
| Governance | Who approves data, communications, security, legal, commercial, and jurisdiction conditions? | Governance Approver |
| Economics | Who monitors full usage, volume variance, alerts, and realized outcome value? | Sponsor with finance/operations |

## Decision criteria

1. **Economic materiality:** Is the current problem large enough to justify implementation and ongoing operation?
2. **Workflow boundedness:** Can the team define what the agent may do, must transfer, and must never do?
3. **Outcome measurement:** Is there a trustworthy baseline and a completed-outcome denominator?
4. **Caller experience:** Can representative callers complete the job without unacceptable friction or deception?
5. **Human fallback:** Are transfer, callback, outage, complaint, and severe-exception paths staffed and tested?
6. **Technical readiness:** Are telephony, source systems, tools, concurrency, observability, and failure recovery viable?
7. **Quality ownership:** Is someone accountable for live sampling, evaluations, regression, and change control?
8. **Governance fit:** Can data, geography, outbound, recording, disclosure, consent, and contract requirements be approved?
9. **Fully loaded economics:** Does value hold after every usage and operating component?
10. **Expansion discipline:** Can the team repeat the pattern without assuming the first case generalizes automatically?

## Objections, fears, and required proof

| Objection or fear | What it is testing | Proof required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “We cannot risk our customer experience on an AI voice.” | Brand, service, and caller acceptance | Representative calls, complaint/repeat-call data, transfer quality, severe-failure limits, and staged volume |
| “The ROI disappears once every cost is included.” | Unit economics | Cost per acceptable completed outcome under normal and peak volume, including implementation, QA, telephony, models, voices, concurrency, fallback, and support |
| “This will become another indefinite pilot.” | Ownership and production readiness | Named sponsor, workflow owner, builder, quality owner, production requirements, dated gates, and a commercial next step |
| “We can build this ourselves.” | Strategic control and total ownership cost | Honest DIY comparison covering real-time stack, telephony, evaluation, observability, on-call support, model change, and opportunity cost |
| “A BPO already gives us a managed service.” | Operational burden and accountability | Clear division of Retell, partner, internal, and frontline responsibilities plus comparable service economics |
| “An error could harm a patient, borrower, driver, or applicant.” | Consequence and safe scope | Bounded intents, prohibited actions, human escalation, identity checks, test evidence, and legal/clinical/regulated review |
| “Compliance claims are too generic.” | Approval quality | Current trust package, data-flow/subprocessor map, retention configuration, BAA/DPA where applicable, communications-law responsibilities, and signed terms |
| “One workflow cannot justify another platform.” | Strategic reuse | A realistic second-workflow or embedded-product plan with marginal cost, reusable assets, and extra governance work |
| “Will humans actually use the handoff?” | Operating adoption | Frontline training, context transfer, queue ownership, callback rules, and feedback loop |

## Proof hierarchy

1. Customer-specific baseline and completed-outcome economics
2. Representative calls using the buyer's data, tools, transfers, accents, languages, and failure conditions
3. Independently reported or directly attributable evidence from a comparable workflow
4. Production operating model with named workflow, technical, builder, quality, frontline, and governance owners
5. Data, communications, security, legal, and contract evidence scoped to the actual use case
6. Load, concurrency, outage, transfer, billing-alert, and rollback evidence
7. Expansion model with incremental economics and operating capacity
8. Generic voice demos, feature lists, unscoped certifications, or vendor headline metrics

| Stage | Executive Sponsor's job | Needed content or evidence | Common stall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem recognition | Make the cost, capacity, service, or revenue problem material | Baseline calculator and consequence-of-inaction brief | Pain is visible but not economically quantified |
| Discovery | Compare hiring, BPO, IVR/CCaaS, DIY, partners, and specialist platforms | Category map, build-versus-buy analysis, relevant workflow cases | Naturalness demo substitutes for operating evaluation |
| Evaluation | Ensure technical testing answers a business question | Representative-call plan, full-cost model, outcome and severe-failure gates | Clean demo data hides integration and exception work |
| Approval | Align technical, workflow, governance, and commercial conditions | Responsibility matrix, data map, trust package, BAA/DPA/terms, rollout boundary | Residency, data use, outbound duties, or accountability stays ambiguous |

## Segment variants

### Variant A — Founder or CEO in a smaller operator

- **Observed pattern:** [Sunshine Loans](https://www.retellai.com/case-study/sunshine-loans-retell-ai-customer-support-automation), [SWTCH](https://www.retellai.com/case-study/how-swtch-keeps-ev-drivers-moving-with-always-on-voice-support-powered-by-retell), [ISpeedToLead](https://www.retellai.com/case-study/ispeedtolead-retell-ai-lead-response-case-study), and [Boatzon](https://www.retellai.com/case-study/how-retell-ai-became-boatzons-top-performing-employee) show founder/CEO-level participation, though evidence quality varies.
- **Role concentration:** Sponsor may also own workflow, billing, workspace administration, and vendor relationship; one engineer or external partner may build.
- **Primary concern:** Stop lost revenue or service leakage quickly without hiring a full team.
- **Best proof:** A live version of the exact workflow, clear per-outcome economics, simple integration, reliable transfer, and an operating plan that fits a small team.
- **Messaging risk:** Do not equate speed with “set and forget” or imply that no technical/QA work is needed.

### Variant B — COO or operating executive

- **Observed pattern:** Pine Park and Spare connect voice automation to provider/frontline capacity, service access, and operating leverage.
- **Role separation:** More likely to rely on product/operations for workflow definition, engineering for systems, and risk functions for approval.
- **Primary concern:** Improve capacity and service without simply moving work into exceptions or customer complaints.
- **Best proof:** Baseline-to-outcome operating evidence, human work reallocation, safe handoff, caller experience, and a staged scale plan.
- **Messaging risk:** Avoid leading with model architecture or raw containment without downstream completion.

### Variant C — CIO or transformation executive

- **Observed pattern:** Everise, MDS, and Anker place a CIO or transformation leader in the visible sponsor position.
- **Primary concern:** Deliver a production AI result that integrates with existing systems and can be governed and repeated.
- **Best proof:** Business case plus architecture, evaluation, access control, data map, reliability model, and explicit business ownership.
- **Messaging risk:** Do not make IT solely accountable for business value or treat assurance as a late-stage checkbox.

### Variant D — Embedded-platform or BPO executive

- **Observed pattern:** [Spare](https://www.retellai.com/case-study/how-sparelabs-put-ai-voice-on-the-front-line-of-public-transit-and-watched-it-take-off), [Cents](https://www.retellai.com/case-study/how-cents-transforms-laundry-operations-with-24-7-voice-automation), and [Everise](https://www.retellai.com/case-study/how-everise-contained-65-of-internal-service-desk-tickets-with-retell-ai) connect Retell to downstream product revenue or multi-client service delivery.
- **Primary concern:** Repeatability, tenant/client configuration, support leverage, quality at scale, product velocity, and margin after metered usage.
- **Best proof:** APIs, templates, bulk operations, client isolation, downstream controls, support model, and marginal economics across many agents.
- **Messaging risk:** Do not reuse a single-enterprise ROI model for an OEM or managed-service business.

## Stakeholder dynamics

### Natural allies

- Workflow Owner with a measurable queue, service, scheduling, collections, or revenue problem
- Technical Owner who can expose integration and operating risk before commitment
- Agent Builder or solution partner capable of getting representative calls live
- Conversation Quality Owner who can convert failures into tests and guard volume expansion
- Frontline managers who gain capacity and own transferred or exceptional work
- Governance Approver engaged before architecture and outbound/data assumptions harden

### Likely tension

- Functional teams that want immediate coverage but have not defined policy or exceptions
- Technical teams asked to own business outcomes without a baseline or process owner
- Finance teams comparing base per-minute price with human wages while omitting implementation and QA
- Governance reviewers brought in after a public or politically committed pilot
- Frontline teams who hear “replacement” while being asked to absorb harder transferred calls
- Builders who optimize containment while the sponsor needs completed outcomes and protected customer experience

## Messaging and content guidance

### Lead with

- One material phone workflow, its current cost or missed outcome, and the human boundary
- Completed business outcomes with a clear denominator
- A narrow production path: representative testing, staged volume, safe handoff, live QA, and named owners
- Fully loaded economics rather than a base cents-per-minute comparison
- How capacity is redirected to complex or licensed work
- Evidence strength: independent, customer-stated, vendor-claimed, or inferred

### Avoid

- “Replace your call center” as the default executive promise
- “Human-like” or low latency as if either proves completion, quality, or ROI
- “Compliant out of the box,” blanket HIPAA/GDPR/TCPA claims, or treating certification as customer approval
- Presenting selected case-study outcomes as averages or guarantees
- Calling containment a business outcome when repeat calls, complaints, failed actions, or cleanup are unknown
- Assuming the quoted executive is the contract signer or renewal owner
- Treating every company above a headcount threshold as a fit

### High-value content formats

- Executive phone-workflow opportunity calculator
- Cost per acceptable completed outcome model
- Hire/BPO/CCaaS/DIY/Retell decision guide
- Pilot charter with success and severe-failure gates
- Human-plus-AI operating model and responsibility map
- Comparable customer evidence brief with evidence classes and denominators
- Board-ready risk, control, and expansion summary
- Embedded-platform or multi-client unit-economics model

## Retell-specific diligence the sponsor should expect

- Public [pricing](https://www.retellai.com/pricing) is componentized; model the configured stack and internal operating cost.
- [Access control](https://docs.retellai.com/accounts/access-control) separates Admin, Developer, and read-only capabilities, but workspace permissions do not map automatically to organizational authority.
- [Testing guidance](https://docs.retellai.com/test/test-overview) distinguishes simulation from real-audio and production QA; a sponsor should require all three where relevant.
- [Concurrency guidance](https://docs.retellai.com/deploy/concurrency) documents a pay-as-you-go default of 20 concurrent calls plus higher-capacity options and related planning.
- Current [Terms of Service](https://www.retellai.com/legal/terms-of-service), effective 2026-06-01, place important consent, disclosure, recording, content, and legal-compliance duties on the customer and warn that AI output may be inaccurate. They prohibit autodial, AI voice, or prerecorded-message calls to 911 and emergency telephone lines, hospital or healthcare-facility emergency lines, patient rooms in hospitals, healthcare facilities, or elderly homes, and paging services. Separately, §8 says the service is not designed for medical, emergency, aviation, nuclear, or other inherently dangerous or safety-critical environments and disclaims liability for those uses. Ordinary healthcare scheduling is not automatically the same category, but still requires use-specific review.
- The same Terms permit Retell, when recording is not opted out, to use Communications and User Content for analytics and for developing, training, and improving AI/ML models included in the service, after the data is de-identified and aggregated. Any narrower commitment must be verified in the buyer's actual written agreement.
- [Compliance documentation](https://docs.retellai.com/general/compliance) describes assurance and agreement options but says Retell does not currently operate services in the EU; geography and processing requirements need customer-specific confirmation.
