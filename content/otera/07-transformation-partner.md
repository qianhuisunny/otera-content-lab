# Otera Persona 07 — Transformation Partner

## Context metadata

- **Buying role:** Shapes, sells, implements, operates, or scales an Otera program with the customer
- **Representative organizations:** Advisory and consulting firms, GSIs, systems integrators, BPOs, regional transformation partners, technology partners, and OEM partners
- **Representative roles:** Managing Partner, Practice Lead, Alliance Lead, Business Director, Account Executive, Transformation Lead, Solution Architect, Delivery Lead, Managed Services Lead
- **Research cutoff:** 2026-08-23
- **Evidence confidence:** High for Otera's published partner motion; medium for partner economics and delivery performance; low for undisclosed contract terms
- **Library index:** [Otera Persona Library](README.md)

## Evidence convention

- **Otera or customer evidence:** Linked or explicitly attributed. Metrics hosted by Otera are treated as vendor-published customer evidence unless an independent source is named.
- **External evidence:** Linked to the named analyst, industry body, regulator, or other authoritative source; it supports broader role context, not Otera performance.
- **Researcher inference:** Unless a statement is linked or explicitly attributed, titles and reporting patterns, operating cadence, pains and workarounds, triggers, JTBD, objections, and stakeholder dynamics are working hypotheses inferred from the documented workflow.

## Persona in one sentence

The transformation partner needs to turn client access and industry or process expertise into repeatable, governed Otera deployments—with clear account ownership, delivery responsibility, customer outcomes, and viable services economics.

## Role boundary

This dossier focuses on the **business and delivery partner** involved in client discovery, transformation, implementation, co-selling, managed services, or expansion.

Otera also describes:

- **Technology partners:** Hyperscalers, model providers, process-mining companies, and ISVs that extend the ecosystem without necessarily owning client operations
- **OEM partners:** Software vendors that embed Otera capabilities in their own offering

Do not treat these three partner types as economically or operationally identical.

## What this persona owns

- Client relationship and account context where the partner is incumbent
- Discovery, process assessment, opportunity qualification, and transformation narrative
- Sponsor coalition and business-case development
- Industry, regulatory, local, and operating-model expertise
- Joint solution design and delivery scope
- Customer change, training, adoption, and sometimes managed operations
- Commercial role: referral, co-sell, gain share, implementation, resale, or OEM motion
- Delivery capacity, utilization, project quality, referenceability, and expansion
- Clear handoff among customer, partner, Otera, cloud, model, and system owners

## Otera's published partner journey

Otera describes a five-stage motion:

1. **Onboard:** Formalize the relationship and access program resources.
2. **Enable:** Train through Otera Academy and role-based certification paths.
3. **Co-Sell:** Build joint go-to-market plans and collaborate on accounts.
4. **Co-Deliver:** Implement and scale autonomous operations together.
5. **Scale:** Replicate success across verticals, business units, and geographies.

This is Otera's program design, not evidence that every partner progresses through every stage.

## Operating cadence

### Account and pipeline cadence

- Identify operational processes with volume, measurable pain, data, and executive sponsorship
- Qualify whether the client has a production path, not only interest in AI
- Align account ownership, opportunity registration, value hypothesis, and next action
- Coordinate Otera subject-matter and technical support

### Delivery cadence

- Discover the workflow, baseline, systems, rules, exceptions, and stakeholders
- Define business case, architecture, control model, implementation scope, and RACI
- Configure and integrate, run in parallel, and move to production
- Manage adoption, exception ownership, outcome measurement, and issue escalation
- Transfer capability or continue managed delivery according to the agreed model

### Scale cadence

- Turn the first result into a reusable industry or process package
- Train additional sellers, consultants, architects, and delivery teams
- Expand within the account and replicate across similar customers
- Monitor services margin, delivery capacity, platform dependency, and customer outcomes

## Core pains

### 1. Clients want production AI but cannot operationalize it

AI demonstrations often stall when they encounter legacy systems, governance, risk, process variability, and organizational ownership.

### 2. Custom agent work is difficult to reuse

Building each client solution from model and cloud primitives can create high engineering effort, weak margins, and long-term maintenance obligations.

### 3. Delivery responsibility becomes ambiguous

The client may not know whether the partner, Otera, cloud provider, model provider, or internal team owns discovery, configuration, integration, controls, support, and outcomes.

### 4. Partner economics depend on value that is hard to baseline

Outcome or gain-share motions require agreed volumes, costs, attribution, measurement, and risk allocation.

### 5. Client trust is at stake

A partner can damage an established relationship by introducing immature technology, overstating autonomy, or underestimating production change.

## Current workarounds

These are **inferences**, not a disclosed inventory for Otera partners.

- Custom consulting and process redesign
- Traditional RPA and workflow implementation
- Partner-built accelerators on top of hyperscaler or model platforms
- Staff augmentation for client AI and integration teams
- BPO or managed operations that preserve labor-intensive delivery
- Isolated proofs of concept with no reusable production layer

## Buying and partnership triggers

- Clients ask for operational AI rather than experimentation
- A repeatable vertical workflow appears across multiple accounts
- Margin pressure makes labor-heavy transformation less attractive
- A regional or mid-market customer lacks internal agent-building capacity
- A partner wants to attach implementation, change, or managed services to a platform
- An incumbent account needs a faster path than custom development
- Cloud marketplace or technology alliances create a viable procurement route
- Named production results make a new autonomous-operations practice credible

## Jobs to be done

### Functional jobs

- Convert client process knowledge and account access into governed production deployments
- Package repeatable industry and workflow expertise around Otera
- Build a defensible business case and sponsor coalition
- Co-sell and co-deliver with clear roles
- Protect delivery quality, margin, and customer outcomes
- Transfer ownership or operate the solution according to the agreed model
- Expand across processes, business units, countries, and similar customers

### Emotional jobs — inference

- Avoid staking a trusted client relationship on immature or ambiguous technology
- Feel confident that Otera will support delivery when the process reaches real exceptions
- Avoid being trapped between vendor promises and customer accountability

### Social and organizational jobs — inference

- Be recognized as the partner that turns AI ambition into measurable operating results
- Build a differentiated practice instead of reselling undifferentiated software
- Demonstrate credible innovation to clients, internal leadership, and delivery teams

## Commercial and delivery motions

Otera publicly describes three commercial motions:

| Motion | Partner role | Main diligence question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transactional | Referral or commercial influence without delivery | How are opportunity ownership, eligibility, timing, and attribution defined? |
| Gain share | Participation connected to measured customer value | How are baseline, causality, exclusions, timing, audit, and downside handled? |
| Deployment | Partner implements as subcontractor or sells and delivers with greater independence | Who owns scope, acceptance, liability, support, and customer outcome? |

Otera also describes throughput-based pricing and says partner models can include co-delivery, implementation, managed operations, and white-label-friendly delivery. It publishes marketing claims about partner economics but does not disclose standard rates or contracts.

Do not invent referral percentages, gain-share formulas, margins, tiers, minimums, exclusivity, deal-registration duration, or liability terms.

## Responsibility model to clarify

| Workstream | Customer | Transformation partner | Otera |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Business outcome and process ownership | Accountable | Advises and helps operationalize | Supports outcome design |
| Discovery and transformation narrative | Provides context and owners | Often leads | Supplies product and domain input |
| Workflow and exception design | Owns rules and acceptance | Facilitates and configures | Provides platform expertise and engineering support |
| Architecture and integration | Owns enterprise standards and systems | Designs or implements | Supports platform-specific integration |
| Risk and compliance approval | Owns acceptance | Prepares evidence and coordinates | Supplies platform assurance and control evidence |
| Configuration and delivery | Participates and accepts | May lead or co-deliver | May supply forward-deployed engineering |
| Change, training, and adoption | Owns organization change | Often leads | Supplies product enablement |
| Production support and optimization | Owns business operation | May provide managed service | Supports platform performance and escalation |

This is a planning model, not a published contract. Every engagement requires a specific RACI.

## Objections and failure fears

| Objection or fear | What it is testing | Proof required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Otera will compete for or take over our client relationship." | Account and channel trust | Account-ownership, communication, registration, renewal, and expansion rules |
| "Our services margin will disappear." | Delivery economics | Implementation model, resource assumptions, reuse, support load, and commercial scenario |
| "We will remain dependent on Otera engineers." | Independent capability | Enablement path, sandbox, documentation, certification, and customer-run or partner-run proof |
| "We carry liability for autonomous outcomes we cannot control." | Responsibility and contractual risk | Authority model, RACI, acceptance, support, insurance, liability, and incident terms |
| "Gain share cannot be measured fairly." | Attribution and auditability | Baseline, metric, counterfactual, exclusions, data access, timing, and dispute process |
| "The platform is not differentiated enough." | Market and account value | Comparable production proof, end-to-end capability, governance, and process-specific assets |
| "The first project will be too custom to repeat." | Scalability | Reusable architecture, agents, templates, industry play, and implementation effort history |
| "Lead flow and internal support will be weak." | Program commitment | Named enablement, presales, account collaboration, escalation, and governance model |

## Proof hierarchy

1. Clear customer–partner–Otera RACI and contract structure
2. Comparable production references and reference calls
3. Repeatable industry or workflow package with transparent custom work
4. Enablement and certification path demonstrated by partner staff
5. Delivery capacity, effort, margin, and support model based on inspectable assumptions
6. Joint value-case and outcome-measurement method
7. Marketing claims about ecosystem scale or partner economics without contractual detail

## Stakeholder dynamics

### Natural allies

- Economic and functional sponsors with measurable operational pain
- Executive champion who needs external delivery capacity
- Technical evaluator and architecture teams
- Risk and procurement functions when evidence is organized early
- Otera forward-deployed and partner teams with explicit boundaries
- Cloud or technology partners that simplify procurement and deployment

### Likely tension

- Customer internal services teams or automation CoEs protecting scope
- Incumbent RPA, BPO, or SI providers
- Procurement challenging attribution, gain share, or overlapping suppliers
- Otera and partner disagreement over account ownership or delivery responsibility
- Delivery teams asked to absorb custom work not reflected in the business case
- Sellers promising production timelines before discovery is complete

## Content strategy

### Lead with

- The partner's client, industry, and transformation value—not software resale
- Joint responsibility for measurable workflow outcomes
- Clear split between partner expertise and Otera platform or engineering support
- Repeatable delivery, governance, and expansion assets
- Transparent commercial and delivery assumptions

### Avoid

- Opening with “resell Otera”
- Promising undisclosed margin, referral, or gain-share economics
- Implying exclusivity or guaranteed lead flow
- Hiding dependence on Otera engineering
- Claiming delivery is effortless because the platform is low-code
- Treating a custom first project as proof of repeatability

### High-value content formats

- Partner qualification and ICP guide
- Industry and process playbooks
- Discovery, viability, and value-case toolkit
- Joint account and stakeholder plan
- Reference architecture and governance pack
- Demo and sandbox environment
- Delivery methodology, RACI, and effort model
- Enablement and certification map
- Commercial-motion explainer with clearly labeled assumptions
- Expansion and managed-services playbook
