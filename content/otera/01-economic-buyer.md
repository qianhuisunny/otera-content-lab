# Otera Persona 01 — Economic Buyer

## Context metadata

- **Buying role:** Funds or authorizes the operating-model change
- **Representative titles:** COO, Chief Claims Officer, Head of Global Business Services, business-unit president, operational P&L owner
- **Research cutoff:** 2026-08-23
- **Evidence confidence:** High for operational pains and desired outcomes; medium for exact title, reporting line, and signature authority

## Evidence convention

- **Otera or customer evidence:** Linked or explicitly attributed. Metrics hosted by Otera are treated as vendor-published customer evidence unless an independent source is named.
- **External evidence:** Linked to the named analyst, industry body, regulator, or other authoritative source; it supports broader role context, not Otera performance.
- **Researcher inference:** Unless a statement is linked or explicitly attributed, titles and reporting patterns, operating cadence, pains and workarounds, triggers, JTBD, objections, and stakeholder dynamics are working hypotheses inferred from the documented workflow.

## Persona in one sentence

The economic buyer is accountable for whether a high-volume operation can improve margin, capacity, service, and control without scaling headcount or replacing its core systems.

Otera states that its largest programs are usually C-level sponsored and commercially structured around operational outcomes rather than seats or modules. That makes **economic accountability for the workflow** more important than any single job title.

## Role boundary

This persona owns or approves the business result. It is not necessarily:

- The daily product user
- The person who configures agents and workflows
- The lead architect or security approver
- The person who first discovered Otera
- The only executive in the buying committee

The economic buyer may also act as the executive champion in a smaller or unusually centralized program, but writers should not assume the roles are identical.

## Typical titles and reporting context

| Title pattern | Likely economic accountability | Common reporting context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chief Operating Officer | Enterprise operating cost, capacity, service, execution risk, and customer experience | CEO or president |
| Chief Claims Officer | Claims cost, cycle time, leakage, quality, regulatory handling, and claimant experience | CEO, COO, or insurance business president |
| Head of GBS / Shared Services | Cost-to-serve, service delivery, process standardization, transformation value, and scale across functions | COO, CFO, or enterprise-services executive |
| Business-unit P&L owner | Margin, growth capacity, service quality, and operating leverage in a defined business | CEO, group executive, or regional president |

**Inference:** Reporting structures vary widely. Target the person who owns the business consequence and can mobilize funding, not a title in isolation.

## What this person owns

- A material operating budget or P&L line
- Service-level performance and customer or client impact
- Capacity planning, staffing pressure, and productivity
- The financial treatment of efficiency, redeployment, growth capacity, and implementation cost
- The decision to make a workflow transformation strategically important
- Executive escalation when technology, risk, procurement, or change-management gates stall
- The decision to expand after the first workflow proves—or fails to prove—its value

## Core pains

### 1. Growth still scales with headcount

More documents, emails, cases, claims, or transactions require more people, overtime, or external support. Peaks expose an operating model with little elastic capacity.

### 2. The backlog becomes a business problem

Queue length and delay stop being internal efficiency metrics when they affect client contracts, claimant recovery, customer retention, cash flow, or regulatory exposure.

### 3. Scarce expertise is spent on administrative work

Experienced claims handlers, finance analysts, or process specialists spend time triaging, rekeying, reconciling, chasing missing information, and preparing routine decisions.

### 4. Prior automation improves steps but not the whole process

OCR, IDP, RPA, rules engines, workflow tools, and internal builds may handle deterministic steps while leaving people to connect systems, interpret context, and resolve exceptions.

### 5. Core-system transformation is too slow or risky

The business cannot wait for a multiyear replacement before improving operational performance. It needs a production layer that can work with existing systems and controls.

## Current workarounds

These are **inferences from the documented pain pattern**, not a published list of Otera customer tools.

- Add permanent headcount, overtime, contractors, BPO capacity, or surge teams
- Build more queues and manual quality checks around existing RPA or IDP
- Narrow automation to easy cases and leave most consequential decisions manual
- Accept seasonal backlogs and slower service as unavoidable
- Delay expansion because operations cannot support additional volume or complexity
- Run AI pilots without changing the production operating model

## Buying triggers

- Backlog, SLA failure, or customer escalation becomes visible at executive level
- Volume growth or demand volatility requires disproportionate hiring
- Experienced employees retire or leave, exposing scarce institutional knowledge
- A client renewal, catastrophe, close cycle, or market expansion exposes process fragility
- Prior RPA, IDP, workflow, or internal AI investment reaches an exception-handling ceiling
- The board or CEO demands measurable P&L impact from AI rather than more experimentation
- Core-system replacement is deferred, creating demand for a non-disruptive overlay
- A first autonomous workflow proves enough value to justify broader expansion

## Jobs to be done

### Functional jobs

- Scale service and transaction capacity without proportional headcount growth
- Reduce backlog, cycle time, and cost per case while preserving quality
- Reserve expert attention for judgment, relationships, and unusual cases
- Turn a fragmented, document-heavy workflow into governed end-to-end execution
- Improve the operating model without waiting for core-system replacement
- Produce a defensible business case with measurable production outcomes

### Emotional jobs — inference

- Reduce the anxiety of being visibly accountable for queues, cost creep, and service failure
- Feel confident that autonomous execution does not mean surrendering control
- Avoid sponsoring another high-profile AI pilot that never changes operations

### Social and organizational jobs — inference

- Demonstrate that operations can enable growth and customer value, not only cut cost
- Give the board, clients, employees, and regulators a credible transformation narrative
- Show that workforce capacity is being redirected deliberately rather than removed indiscriminately

## Decision criteria

1. **Materiality:** Is the selected workflow large or costly enough to justify enterprise change?
2. **Measurability:** Is there an agreed baseline and a small set of outcomes the buyer owns?
3. **Production credibility:** Is the proposed result a real operating workflow rather than a demo or disconnected proof of concept?
4. **Time to value:** Can the organization reach production quickly enough to matter without hiding implementation work?
5. **Risk control:** Can humans define boundaries, inspect decisions, and intervene in high-risk cases?
6. **Core-system compatibility:** Can the program avoid a disruptive rip-and-replace requirement?
7. **Scalability:** Can the result repeat across higher volume, new workflows, or multiple countries?
8. **Workforce transition:** Is there a credible plan for experts, exception handling, and change adoption?

## Objections and failure fears

| Objection or fear | What it is testing | Proof required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "This will create unacceptable claims, financial, or compliance errors." | Accountability for consequential decisions | Decision traces, confidence thresholds, human override, exception routing, and agreed acceptance metrics |
| "This will become another pilot with no P&L effect." | Production and business-case credibility | Named owner, baseline, bounded workflow, implementation milestones, and live outcome measures |
| "We already invested in automation and core platforms." | Incremental value and architectural fit | Clear capability boundary, coexistence with existing systems, and evidence on exception-heavy cases |
| "The savings are theoretical." | Measurement integrity | Buyer's own volumes, cost, quality, exception, and delay baseline plus post-launch reporting |
| "The workforce disruption will damage service." | Change-management and customer risk | Capacity-redeployment plan, new user roles, training, and protected escalation paths |
| "The vendor outcome is not comparable to our process." | Reference relevance | Proof matched by industry, workflow, scale, regulatory context, and starting baseline |

## Proof hierarchy

From strongest to weakest for this persona:

1. Measured production outcome from a comparable named customer
2. Buyer's own baseline-to-target model with agreed ownership
3. A workflow-level demonstration using representative documents and exceptions
4. A phased production plan with business, technology, risk, and workforce responsibilities
5. Analyst or partner validation with transparent methodology
6. Generic product claims, awards, logos, or model benchmarks

## Buyer-journey role

| Stage | Economic buyer's role | Content needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem recognition | Makes the operational constraint strategically visible | Cost-of-status-quo brief and workflow benchmarks |
| Workflow selection | Confirms materiality and assigns an accountable functional owner | Use-case qualification and baseline worksheet |
| Business case | Sponsors or approves benefits logic and funding | P&L model, capacity treatment, risk assumptions, and outcome definition |
| Evaluation | Tests business fit while delegating technical and risk diligence | Comparable proof, outcome scorecard, and executive decision memo |
| Commercial approval | Judges price and delivery risk against expected operating value | Commercial structure, implementation scope, responsibilities, and downside case |

## Stakeholder dynamics

### Natural allies

- Functional champion who owns the workflow and baseline
- CIO, CDO, Chief AI Officer, or automation leader who can make the program production-ready
- Finance or FP&A leader who validates benefit treatment
- Customer-experience or client-service leader where delay affects retention
- Transformation partner when internal delivery capacity is constrained

### Likely tension

- Technical teams when business urgency outruns architecture and ownership clarity
- Risk functions when straight-through processing is pushed ahead of control evidence
- Frontline leaders or employee representatives when change is framed only as labor removal
- Procurement when outcome claims are not converted into inspectable commercial assumptions
- Existing platform owners when Otera is positioned as replacement rather than a defined complementary layer

## Content strategy

### Lead with

- The operational constraint and the consequence: backlog, cycle time, cost, capacity, service, or risk
- A named, comparable production result with the metric's source and baseline
- The role of governed exceptions and human authority
- A credible path from one workflow to measurable expansion
- The workforce-capacity outcome, not only the automation rate

### Avoid

- Opening with models, agents, no-code features, or technical architecture
- Promising a percentage saving before inspecting the workflow baseline
- Treating every C-level executive as the economic buyer
- Equating high automation with indiscriminate workforce reduction
- Presenting Otera-published metrics as guaranteed or independently audited
- Calling a demo or isolated proof of concept an operating-model transformation

### High-value content formats

- One-page operational business case
- Baseline and target KPI worksheet
- Comparable customer outcome story
- End-to-end workflow and exception map
- Workforce transition and operating-model brief
- Executive scale roadmap across processes, entities, or countries
